## Collaboration history

1. The creator proposed an AI sound novel influenced by Twitch viewers. Codex reviewed feasibility, pacing, competition fit and similar existing projects.
2. The creator changed the concept to a timed dungeon escape party game: viewers inhabit weak goblins, cooperate, ambush or prey on each other. Command-based goblin control and canned proximity speech were discussed.
3. Codex generated three character concept illustrations. The creator used Meshy to generate and rig the AI goblin, viewer goblin and hero models. Codex inspected the supplied GLB structures and animation clips. Meshy work was performed by the creator; it was not generated by the game's runtime.
4. Codex implemented a local Three.js/Vite game, movement, combat, AI goblins, timer and initial objective.
5. Play feedback led to an FPS hero camera, narrow corridors, higher walls, goblin-only tunnels and pathfinding. The viewer view was agreed to be overhead, but is not yet implemented.
6. The creator requested faster directional sword attacks and impact feedback. Codex added view-directed melee, sparks and a hit marker.
7. Input feedback led to pointer lock, immediate left-click attacks, hold-to-attack, sensitivity adjustment and pause on cursor release. Embedded-browser pointer lock was observed to fail; full FPS play requires a supporting browser.
8. The creator suggested temporarily sealing burrows. Codex added E-to-seal, timed reopening and AI digging with destructible sand durability.
9. The objective changed to finding treasure and returning to the entrance. Codex added explored-area automapping, starting equipment roulette, recovery items, concealed passages and climbing connections.
10. The creator proposed potion recovery, a passive recovery ring and Soul Eater kill recovery. Codex implemented the three recovery types and guaranteed a healing-related starting slot.
11. The creator requested guns. Codex added a switchable pistol, finite ammunition, reload, recoil, muzzle flash and hit feedback.
12. Play feedback identified excessive FPS combat and constant pursuit. Codex implemented patrol, sight/hearing-based awareness, investigation, pursuit, search and return behavior.
13. A browser shortcut conflict was discovered during play: Ctrl+W closed the tab. Input was corrected. The creator then specified Shift sneak and movement-direction double-tap sprint; Codex implemented and tested them.
14. The creator requested unlimited starting-equipment rerolls for 30 seconds. Codex implemented a fixed deadline and early confirmation.
15. The creator noted the map was still a maze. Codex acknowledged that the spatial redesign had not been completed, then replaced it with seven authored rooms, furnishings, workshop lever and locked treasury. A separate development overview was rendered for visual checking.
16. The creator requested Space jump, low-obstacle traversal and trap-room gameplay. Codex implemented grounded jumping with gravity, low bone/scrap traversal and pressure plates with warning/active/reset phases. Build and physics/collision/trap-state checks passed. Full FPS timing remains unverified in the embedded browser.

### 26. Respawning as a young goblin

- Unified viewer death handling for hero attacks, goblin hunting, and AI retaliation. After 10 seconds, the player respawns as a small young goblin with 3 HP; after another 20 seconds, it grows into one rank below its pre-death form. A normal goblin returns to normal. EXP resets to the starting value of the resulting form; lifetime renown is retained.
- Young goblins cannot be hunted by other goblins and cannot hunt goblins, train, or evolve themselves. They can move, weakly attack the hero, and dig, but digging awards no EXP during childhood. The hero can still damage them.
- Dying again during childhood preserves the planned growth form. If growth would trap the goblin inside a shortcut, it returns to the respawn point. The respawn point was the existing training grounds; a dedicated cradle room did not yet exist.
- Added body-size and name displays on the hero side, respawn/growth countdowns on the viewer side, and exclusion of protected goblins from target selection.
- Verified demotion from normal goblin through Lord, repeated death, the 10-second respawn, and protection against queued goblin hunts and AI retaliation. All 15 tests, including existing tests, passed.

### 28. Role-rotation lobby for up to six players

### 42. Preserve AUTO across death and respawn (2026-11-03 / v0.1.12)

- Report: a goblin switched from direct control to AUTO sometimes returned to direct control after respawning.
- Identified a reproducible input condition: gameplay input remained enabled while dead. A canvas click, movement key, or jump could change the client-side mode even though the host rejected gameplay commands for a dead actor. After respawn, periodic direct packets then disabled host-side AUTO.
- Added a living-actor input guard while retaining menus and camera controls. Clear held keyboard, attack, and touch state on both death and revival. Ignore repeated movement key events after clearing until a fresh press; Shift alone no longer takes over AUTO. Rejoining an already autonomous actor also restores the AUTO UI from host state. Fresh movement or attack input while alive still switches to direct control.
- A new input regression failed before the fix and passed afterward. Host tests also cover ignored dead-actor commands and two consecutive respawns. All 119 tests, the production build, and HTTP/game WebSocket/lobby-chat smoke checks passed. In the real goblin UI, clicking and pressing W/Space while dead preserved AUTO; revival as a young goblin resumed autonomous movement. Physical-phone testing and public deployment were not performed.
